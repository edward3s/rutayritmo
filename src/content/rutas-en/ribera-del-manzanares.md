There are days when the plan calls for 45 easy minutes and you do not feel like driving to the mountains. For those days there is the Manzanares riverside: flat, accessible and easy to dose. It is not the most spectacular route, but it is the one that shows up most often in my week.

## The route

The route follows the promenade beside the river, between bridges and green spaces. It is an out-and-back or a loop, depending on where you start. The surface is asphalt with some packed dirt sections; there is nothing technical.

Choose a starting point with a fountain and restrooms if you are going out for more than 40 minutes. On weekends there are plenty of people walking and cyclists; start early or go late if you want to avoid obstacles.

## Profile and pace

Practically flat. It is ideal for easy runs, for walk-run if you are just starting, or for short intervals on wide straights. The goal sets the pace, not the terrain: you can run 6:30 min/km one day and 5:00 in a quality session.

If you are just starting, alternate running and walking. The guide on [starting to run in 8 weeks](/en/diario/empezar-a-correr-en-8-semanas/) fits perfectly here. If you already have a base, use the straight for [400 m intervals](/en/diario/series-400-vs-1000/) or for a [zone 2](/en/diario/zona-2-correr-despacio/) run with no gradient forcing you to slow down.

## Water and fueling

There are fountains at several points along the promenade, but not all of them work all year. Carry a small bottle if you will be out for more than an hour. In summer, avoid the middle of the day: the asphalt and the lack of shade make themselves felt.

## Getting there

Metro and bus reach several parts of the route. If you drive, there are park-and-ride lots nearby, but the center gets complicated. The bike or public transport is usually the most comfortable option.

## When to go

All year. In summer, early or at sunset. In winter, at midday if it is cold. After rain, some sections may have puddles; it is not a technical route, but water hides paving stones.

## Safety

It is an urban, busy area. Cross at marked crossings, do not invade the bike lane and stay alert at junctions. If you run at night, wear visible clothing. And if you wear headphones, keep one ear free: some cyclists warn you late.

It is the least epic route in the diary and, probably, the one that will serve you best for building the habit. Big mountains are good for photos; the riverside is good for Tuesdays.
