The reservoir loop can help you add kilometers on track, but the surface, the shade and the wind exposure vary depending on the section you choose. Check the route and the current state of the access before heading out.

## The route

Start from the reservoir parking area. The route circles the reservoir on track, with some packed dirt sections and short climbs. It is hard to get lost if you follow the main line, but there are side paths leading to viewpoints and fishing spots; do not take them if you do not know where they go.

The track has some potholes and loose stone at the edges. It is not technical, but it is not a road either: watch your footing when you are tired.

## Profile and pace

It is a route for running at an easy pace, not for chasing intervals. The profile rolls gently up and down; the climbing adds up without any big ramps. A pace of 6:00-6:30 min/km is comfortable for a runner with some base. If you go in a group, the pace can easily spike on the flat sections; remember that the goal is to accumulate time, not to race.

## Water and fueling

There are no fountains. Carry water from the car and protect yourself from the sun. In summer, start early: the open track turns into an oven from eleven o'clock onward. The [hydration](/en/diario/hidratacion-en-carreras-populares/) guide has a simple way to work out what you lose.

## Getting there

Drive to El Atazar on the M-133. There are parking areas near the reservoir, but they are not unlimited. Respect the signs and do not block emergency access.

## When to go

Autumn and spring are the best times. In summer, head out at dawn and avoid the middle of the day. In winter it can be very windy and cold in the open areas; bring a windproof layer.

## Safety

The access road has curves and cyclists. If you are on a bike, share the road carefully; if you are running, do not cross without visibility. There are areas with no phone coverage. Carry a charged phone and tell someone your route.

If you are training for a [half marathon](/en/diario/primera-media-maraton/), this loop is a good setting for a 12-14 km long run at a controlled pace. Do not turn it into a race: the track invites you to speed up, but the plan rules.
