This loop is one of those routes that look simple on the map and then remind you that the mountains are not an urban park. There is no serious technical difficulty, but there is constant elevation gain, sections of broken track and a long climb that makes itself felt in the second half.

Foot traffic, mud and the state of the path change with the season and the rain. Check local conditions before planning your outing; your pace will depend on the terrain and the real elevation of the track you choose.

## The route

Start from the area around the Cercedilla station. The first section is wide track and works as a warm-up. Then you enter a trail with roots, loose rock and a few gradients that force you to walk. The route passes by the reservoirs and alternates pine shade with open areas.

There is not perfect signage at every junction. Carry the downloaded track and check the forks: it is easy to take a longer variant without noticing. On the rocky sections, walk if you need to; there is no prize for running all of it.

## Profile and pace

The profile accumulates most of the elevation gain in the first half. The highest point is around 1,300 m and comes after a sustained climb. The descent is not technical, but it is long: do not let yourself go if you are not used to it, because your quads will pay for it the next day.

A pace of 7:00-7:30 min/km on the runnable sections is reasonable for an everyday runner. If you are going to walk the hills, total time gets close to 2 h 30 min. It is not a route for chasing a personal best.

## Water and fueling

There are no fountains on the route. Carry 1.5 liters if it is hot and some food for the second half. The guide on [hydration in popular races](/en/diario/hidratacion-en-carreras-populares/) explains how to work out what you sweat; in the mountains, wind and altitude can fool you.

## Getting there

Cercanías C-8 to Cercedilla and a few minutes on foot to the start. By car, there is parking in the village, but it fills up on weekends. Arrive before 8:30 if you do not want to drive around.

## When to go

Spring and autumn are the best seasons. In summer, head out at dawn and carry more water than you think you need. In winter there can be ice and snow on the upper sections; check the state of the paths and do not improvise with road shoes.

## Safety

Phone coverage is patchy. Tell someone your route and schedule, carry a charged phone and a light windproof layer. There is livestock in some areas: do not bother it and close the gates. And remember that in the National Park, hiking off the marked paths may be restricted.

If you are just starting, this route may be too much for a first outing. Take a look at the [starting to run](/en/diario/empezar-a-correr-en-8-semanas/) plan first and save the 18 km for when you have a base. As for shoes, a trail shoe with some grip is more comfortable than a road one, though it is not essential if the day is dry.
