The marathon is not a long half marathon. It is a test of endurance, patience and logistics. The most common mistake is not running too little in the last month: it is arriving more fatigued than you should be, having run your long runs too fast, or trying a new hydration strategy on race day.

If you have already run a half marathon and have been logging 30-40 kilometers a week for several weeks, you have a base to start from. If not, it's better to go through the [half marathon](/en/diario/primera-media-maraton/) first.

> **In short:** 16 weeks, three or four runs, one long run and one quality session. The longest run does not go beyond 32 km. Volume drops in the last week. The goal is to reach the start line fresh, not exhausted.

## The 16-week plan

| Week | Long run | Quality | Approximate volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 18 km | 3 × 8 min at threshold | 35-40 km |
| 2 | 20 km | 4 × 8 min at threshold | 38-44 km |
| 3 | 22 km | 3 × 10 min at threshold | 40-46 km |
| 4 | 16 km | Easy | 30-35 km |
| 5 | 24 km | 2 × 15 min at threshold | 42-48 km |
| 6 | 26 km | 5 × 8 min at marathon pace | 45-50 km |
| 7 | 28 km | 3 × 10 min at threshold | 46-52 km |
| 8 | 20 km | Easy | 35-40 km |
| 9 | 30 km | 2 × 20 min at marathon pace | 48-55 km |
| 10 | 32 km | 4 × 10 min at threshold | 50-56 km |
| 11 | 24 km | Easy | 40-45 km |
| 12 | 30 km | 3 × 10 min at marathon pace | 48-54 km |
| 13 | 20 km | Easy | 35-40 km |
| 14 | 14 km | 2 × 6 min easy | 25-30 km |
| 15 | 10 km | 20 min easy | 18-22 km |
| 16 | Race | — | 10-15 km |

Weeks 4, 8 and 13 are down weeks. If a long run goes badly, don't repeat it the next day: move on to the next one and adjust the volume.

## The three rules

**1. The long run is not a race.** Run it 30 to 60 seconds per kilometer slower than your goal pace. If you finish unable to talk, you have trained something else. The final part can include a few kilometers at marathon pace, but not the whole run.

**2. Volume rises slowly.** Increase by no more than 10% from one week to the next and take a down week every three or four. The body needs time to adapt; rushing is injury.

**3. Practice nutrition and hydration.** Don't try new gels, drinks or breakfast on race day. Use long runs to test what you tolerate and when. The guide to [what to eat before a long run](/en/diario/comer-antes-de-un-rodaje-largo/) and the [hydration guide](/en/diario/hidratacion-en-carreras-populares/) give you the framework.

## The wall

The famous wall appears when muscle glycogen and available glucose run out. It is not a lack of character. You can delay it by eating carbohydrates during the race, starting at a conservative pace and having trained enough volume.

For most recreational runners, 30-60 grams of carbohydrates per hour starting at 45-60 minutes is a good starting point, provided you have tried it before. Take small sips and don't drink too much. If you start to feel nauseous, dizzy or confused, stop and get help: it may be something more serious than the wall.

## Race week

Reduce volume, keep your usual food and prepare the logistics. The day before, have a carbohydrate dinner you already know; no experiments. Sleep as much as you can: the night before a race is usually poor and ruins nothing if the week has been decent.

On race day, eat breakfast 2-3 hours before, arrive early and warm up for 10 minutes. Start slower than your body wants. The first 10 kilometers should feel like a comfortable training run. If you're already short of breath at kilometer 5, you started too fast.

## After the marathon

Recovering from a marathon takes longer than from a half. Walk, hydrate, eat and rest. Don't run again until you can go down stairs without thinking about it. The first week, walk; the second, try an easy jog; the third, reassess.

The marathon teaches you more about patience than about speed. If you finish, you have already done something most people don't attempt. If you don't finish, you have also learned something you can't buy. Next time, the plan will be better.
