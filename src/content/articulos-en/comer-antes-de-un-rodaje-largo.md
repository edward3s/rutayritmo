The question isn't "what superfood should I eat before running," but "what can I digest well while doing what I have to do." A 90-minute easy run doesn't need a marathon strategy. A three-hour long run does. And in both cases, the best plan is the one you have already tested.

> **In short:** eat easily digestible carbohydrates 2-3 hours before; if you can't, have something small 60-90 minutes before. Keep fat and fiber low in the meal before. Don't try anything new on race day.

## The hours before

| Time before | What to have | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 3-4 h | Full meal with carbohydrates | Rice, chicken, cooked vegetables, fruit |
| 2-3 h | Light meal | Toast with turkey, banana, yogurt |
| 60-90 min | Small snack | Banana, bar, bread with honey |
| 30 min | Only if you need it | Gel or half a banana, with water |

There is no universal meal. If you head out at seven in the morning, you may not be able to eat three hours before. In that case, have a good dinner the night before and something small when you get up. If you run after lunch, leave at least two hours and choose something light.

## What to avoid before heading out

- Very fatty meals: they take longer to empty from the stomach.
- Too much fiber: legumes, cruciferous vegetables, and whole grains can cause bloating.
- Spicy food and alcohol: they don't help a nervous stomach.
- Large amounts of caffeine if you don't usually use it.

This isn't a list of permanent bans. It's a list of things worth testing in training, not on race day.

## Before a long run

For 90 minutes or more, the previous dinner matters. A plate of pasta or rice with a protein source and little fat usually works. Breakfast, if you head out early, can be toast with honey, a banana, and a mild coffee. If you tolerate gels well, take one for the second half; if not, try dates, gummy candy, or a small bar.

The amount of carbohydrate depends on duration and your tolerance. For a 2-3 hour long run, some people need 30-60 grams per hour; others tolerate less. Practice it in training, not on the day of your [first half marathon](/en/diario/primera-media-maraton/).

## After running

You don't need a recovery shake if your next meal is an hour away. Eat something with carbohydrate and protein within the next hour: yogurt with fruit, toast with eggs, rice with tuna. If you train twice a day, the window is shorter and recovery matters more.

## The stomach can be trained

Digestive tolerance improves with practice. If gels always upset your stomach in races, try different brands, textures, and timings. It can also help to take small amounts from the start instead of waiting until hunger appears. The stomach isn't a machine that switches on all at once.

If the problem is recurring, consult a sports dietitian. Nutrition is individual, and one runner's solutions may not work for another.

## The rule that never fails

Nothing new on race day. Not shoes, not gels, not breakfast, not socks. Innovation is tested at home, on a normal run, with time to regret it. And if one day the food doesn't go as planned, it's no big deal: your body has reserves for more than you think.
