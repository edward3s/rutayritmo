The first run isn't won on the first outing. It's won the week you go out again after a bad day, a heavy meal, or a morning when you felt like doing nothing at all. This plan is built for that: so you reach week eight without getting injured or coming to hate running.

> **In short:** three sessions a week, walk-run, able to hold a conversation, and two strength sessions. If a week doesn't go to plan, repeat it. The plan doesn't fall apart because of that.

## Before week one

You don't need 180-euro shoes or a GPS watch. You need athletic shoes in good condition, a safe route with sidewalks or a park, and thirty free minutes three times a week. If you haven't moved in years, have a heart condition, or have an injury that still hurts, check with a healthcare professional first.

The route matters more than it seems. Start with a flat, closed loop with crosswalks and no construction. If the loop passes in front of your home, even better: the barrier to going out is lower.

## The plan, week by week

The pattern is easy running and walking to recover. Don't look at your pace: look at your breathing. If you can't say a short sentence while running, you're going too fast.

| Week | Pattern | Approximate time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 min running / 2 min walking, 8 times | 24 min |
| 2 | 2 min running / 2 min walking, 7 times | 28 min |
| 3 | 3 min running / 2 min walking, 6 times | 30 min |
| 4 | 5 min running / 2 min walking, 4 times | 28 min |
| 5 | 8 min running / 2 min walking, 3 times | 30 min |
| 6 | 10 min running / 1 min walking, 3 times | 33 min |
| 7 | 15 min running / 1 min walking, 2 times | 32 min |
| 8 | 20-30 min continuous running | 20-30 min |

Do the three sessions on non-consecutive days. One example that works: Tuesday, Thursday, and Sunday. If you can't make a day, move the session; don't pile it onto the next day.

## How to know you're at the right pace

The right pace when you start is uncomfortably slow. You should be able to talk in short sentences, not recite a paragraph. If you can only manage one word, slow down or walk. That conversational intensity is what builds your aerobic base; you can read more about it in the [zone 2](/en/diario/zona-2-correr-despacio/) guide.

Don't use a friend's pace as a reference. What's an easy jog for that person may be an all-out run for you. Your watch's heart rate also misleads you at first: heart rate spikes with heat, coffee, or stress.

## Strength twice a week

You don't need a gym. Two 15-minute sessions with simple exercises reduce the risk of knee and tendon trouble and improve running economy:

- Bodyweight squat: 2 sets of 8-12 reps.
- Glute bridge: 2 sets of 10-12, holding for a second at the top.
- Calf raise: 2 sets of 12-15, slowly.
- Plank: 2 sets of 20-30 seconds, without holding your breath.

Do them after running or on a different day. If an exercise hurts, don't push through it: change the range or get it checked.

## The three most common mistakes

**1. Running every session all out.** The feeling that "if you don't finish gasping, it doesn't count" is the fastest shortcut to injury. Most of your volume should be easy.

**2. Skipping weeks.** If you jump to week 5 because you feel good, your body may handle one or two sessions and send you the bill later. Repeating a week isn't losing time.

**3. Comparing your progress to social media.** Nobody posts the day they walked for twenty minutes. Your only useful comparison is your previous week.

## If something hurts

Vague muscle soreness after a session: normal, especially at the start. Sharp pain, a limp, pain that wakes you at night, or pain that keeps getting worse: stop. You don't train through pain that changes the way you run. In the [knee pain](/en/diario/dolor-de-rodilla-cuando-parar/) guide I explain which signals deserve a consultation and which usually respond to reducing the load.

## After week eight

If you've reached 30 minutes of continuous running, you already have a base. Keep three sessions a week for a month: two short and easy, one a little longer. After that you can add a fourth short session or start looking calmly at a [first half marathon](/en/diario/primera-media-maraton/).

Don't chase a fast 5K yet. First, make running a habit. Speed is a consequence of consistency, not the other way around.
