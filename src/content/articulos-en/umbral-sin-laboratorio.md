Threshold has become a word used for everything: to justify a run that came out fast, to sell a plan, and to explain why a Sunday hurts. In reality, it is a fairly specific intensity: the point at which the body starts accumulating lactate faster than it clears it and breathing becomes hard to sustain.

You don't need a blood test to approximate it. You need a flat road, a watch, and the honesty not to go all out in the first minute.

> **In short:** threshold is the intensity you could hold for roughly an hour in a race. You train it with sessions of 20-40 accumulated minutes, once a week, not on every run.

## What threshold feels like

Breathing is hard but rhythmic. You can say a three- or four-word sentence, but you can't hold a conversation. Your legs are working, but there's none of the sharp burn of a sprint. If you look at your watch, it feels like you could hold that pace for half an hour, not two hours.

That description is more useful than a heart rate percentage. Heart rate lags behind, changes with heat, and varies from one day to the next. Pace and breathing are better anchors for day-to-day training.

## The 20-minute test

Warm up with 15 easy minutes, including four 20-second pickups. Then run 20 minutes at the highest intensity you can hold evenly, without sprinting at the end. Note your average pace and how you felt.

That average pace is a reasonable approximation of your threshold. It isn't a lab result, and it doesn't need to be. If the test goes badly —wind, heat, a tired day— repeat it in two weeks. Don't adjust your whole plan around one strange morning.

## How to train it

A typical threshold session might be:

- **Beginner:** 3 × 6 minutes at threshold pace, with 2 minutes of easy jogging between intervals.
- **Intermediate:** 3 × 8 minutes, with 2 minutes of recovery.
- **Advanced:** 2 × 15 minutes or 20-30 continuous minutes.

The key is to accumulate quality time, not to add intervals until your body says enough. If the last interval comes out much slower than the first, you started too fast or the session was too long for where you are right now.

Once a week is enough for most recreational runners. The rest of your volume should be easy, as explained in the [zone 2 guide](/en/diario/zona-2-correr-despacio/). If you add two threshold sessions and keep running your easy runs fast, you aren't training more: you're accumulating fatigue.

## Threshold is not the same as intervals

[400 or 1,000-meter intervals](/en/diario/series-400-vs-1000/) target something else: speed, aerobic power, or running economy. Threshold is more sustained and more controlled. You can run intervals at 5K pace and call it "threshold work," but you'll be training a different quality and arriving at the week more tired.

| Session | Intensity | Typical duration | How it feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy run | Full conversation | 30-90 min | Comfortable |
| Threshold | Short sentences | 20-40 min accumulated | Controlled but hard |
| Intervals | Very hard | 20-30 min with pauses | Out of breath at the end |
| 10K race | All out | 40-60 min | Limit |

## The mistakes that cost the most

**Doing the test and then always training at that pace.** The test is for guidance, not for turning every run into a time trial.

**Confusing threshold with "comfortably fast."** If you can talk through the whole interval, you're probably below it. If you can't say anything, you're above it.

**Doing it on a full stomach or in the heat.** Threshold is demanding. Pick a day with reasonable conditions and don't schedule it after a long run.

## When to reassess

Every six to eight weeks, or when a pace that used to be hard starts to feel comfortable. You don't reassess every week: the body needs time to adapt, and how you feel changes more than your physiology does.

If you're training for a [half marathon](/en/diario/primera-media-maraton/), threshold is one of the highest-value sessions in the plan. If you're just starting out, build your base first and leave threshold for later. Haste is the only kind of training that produces no adaptation.
