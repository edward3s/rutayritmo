Few things have been repeated as often in running as "stretch before and after." The phrase sounds like advice from an old-school coach, but it mixes two different things: warming up and stretching. One prepares the body; the other restores mobility. Doing them in the wrong order won't injure you, but it won't make you invincible either.

> **In short:** before running, dynamic movement; afterward, gentle stretching if you feel like it. Prolonged static stretching before an intense session isn't the best idea.

## Before running: warm up, don't stretch

A useful warm-up lasts five to eight minutes and raises body temperature, activates the muscles, and prepares the joints. You don't need a forty-minute mobility class. This is enough:

- Easy jog for 3-4 minutes.
- Leg swings forward and back, 10 per side.
- Walking lunges, 8 per leg.
- Gentle high knees, 20 seconds.
- Small ankle hops, 15 seconds.
- Two 15-20 second accelerations at a brisk pace.

If you're going to do [intervals](/en/diario/series-400-vs-1000/) or a [threshold](/en/diario/umbral-sin-laboratorio/) test, lengthen the warm-up and add two more strides. The goal is to reach the first rep with your body awake, not with your legs still asleep.

## Static stretching before running

Holding a position for 30-60 seconds before an intense session can temporarily reduce your ability to produce force. For an easy jog it isn't dramatic, but it doesn't add much either. If it relaxes you, do it after the warm-up and without forcing it; if you're racing or doing intervals, leave it for the end.

The evidence doesn't support the idea that stretching on its own prevents injuries either. What works best is training strength, increasing load progressively, and sleeping enough. Stretching is a complement, not a shield.

## After running: gentle mobility

After your run, your body is warm and it's a good time to recover range of motion. You don't need pain or bouncing. Try:

- Wall calf stretch: 20-30 seconds per side.
- Standing quad stretch: 20-30 seconds per side.
- Seated hamstring stretch: 20-30 seconds per side.
- Hip mobility on the floor: 8-10 reps per side.

Breathe slowly and don't push to the limit. If you feel a sharp twinge, come out of the position. Stretching shouldn't hurt.

## What to do if you have an injury

If there's pain in your heel when you get up, in your knee when you go down stairs, or in the sole of your foot, aggressive stretching can make the area worse. In those cases, you first adjust the load and assess strength; then you decide which mobility work makes sense. You can read the [plantar fasciitis](/en/diario/fascitis-plantar-senales/) and [knee pain](/en/diario/dolor-de-rodilla-cuando-parar/) guides for guidance, but an individual assessment is something a professional does.

## A five-minute routine

If you don't want to complicate things, this routine covers the basics after running:

1. Wall calf stretch, 30 seconds per side.
2. Standing quad stretch, 30 seconds per side.
3. Seated hamstring stretch, 30 seconds per side.
4. Hip flexor stretch in a lunge, 30 seconds per side.
5. Slow breathing, one minute.

It isn't mandatory. If you skip it one day, nothing happens. Consistency in running matters more than perfection in your mobility routine.
